**MegaMoE communication latency: prioritized exploration — 2026-09-17**

Recommendation: optimize **which warp must observe completion, which bytes the consumer actually needs, and which ready work can proceed**. The remaining problem is not adequately described by push versus pull or by instruction count. These are exploration proposals, not measured improvements. This review changed only this file; no experiments were launched.

Evidence anchor: the matched concurrent-startup experiment recovered history-like compute (25.088 µs median versus 25.120) but still started MMA later (15.264 versus 9.856 µs from metadata start), with ordinary kernel means 40.8800 versus 38.1344 µs. The newer release-publication ordinary results recorded in the project docs are 40.7136 versus 40.9600 µs for its parent and 37.5360 for history; that small improvement does not close the gap. Sender warp-0 drain→publication issue spans 2.464–4.640 µs, **including other-warp waits and joins**, so it is not a measurement of fence cost or NVLink RTT. [Experiment memory](CODEX_PROJECT_push_dispatch.md:3169).

The completion distinction matters: a push can reach the receiver before its issuer observes completion. A full issuer wait followed by a separate remote flag places completion feedback and another outbound notification on the consumer's dependency chain. An ordinary store need not stall immediately at issue; dependencies, fences, joins, queue pressure and retirement determine where its latency becomes exposed.

| Required next action | Necessary milestone |
|---|---|
| Issue independent work | Issue/commit; no completion wait solely for this purpose |
| Overwrite a TMA source slot | Source reads complete: `wait_group.read`, with correct group-to-slot accounting |
| Publish that a TMA destination is usable | Destination completion plus the required publication/proxy ordering |
| Consume an in-band payload | Validate every consumed transport unit under the protocol |
| Reuse a receive generation | Previous consumption and clearing/credit complete |

`commit_group` creates a per-thread group; it is not a visibility event. Another warp cannot drain that thread's groups. `.read` does not certify destination writes. `st.async.release`/`red.async.release` provide nonblocking generic-proxy publication on SM100+, but do not automatically complete preceding TMA operations. [PTX bulk completion](https://docs.nvidia.com/cuda/parallel-thread-execution/index.html#data-movement-and-conversion-instructions-cp-async-bulk-wait-group), [async reduction](https://docs.nvidia.com/cuda/parallel-thread-execution/index.html#parallel-synchronization-and-communication-instructions-red-async).

1. **Give independent completion domains independent issuers and publishers. Highest-priority bounded experiment.**

   Keep the issuer responsible for its own bulk groups. Replace the four-row/all-peer completion join with independently publishable row/peer subsets where possible. An issuer commits a bounded window, overlaps address/SF/next-transfer preparation, waits only when its oldest required publication or staging reuse demands it, and publishes immediately. Use warp-uniform `red.async.release.sys` for shared counters, or investigate `st.async.release.sys` for single-writer epoch/tail slots. Other warps perform useful work while that issuer waits.

   CuTe's exchange path already has one destination and one issuer; its payload path still joins all pusher warps. Our previous divergent-lane async fanout became an ELECT/broadcast/UREDGR loop and regressed. The new variable must be **ownership and parallel publication**, not the mnemonic. Source staging may still be shared, but its reuse must wait for all readers. At rows32, eight CTAs × four warps give one row per warp: deeper next-row buffering alone has nothing to overlap. Measure receiver readiness and last sender retirement together. [Exchange/pusher](../cutedsl_megamoe/next/sources/kernel_src/rubin/inference/mega/token_comm_gen_specialized.py:1183), [failed async fanout](CODEX_PROJECT_push_dispatch.md:1553).

2. **Make receiver readiness independent of sender completion feedback. Highest upside; protocol work.**

   Explore a receiver-side validator for a bounded row/K window: keep TMA push and TMA gather, validate every payload transport word in that window, then publish a local readiness certificate for gather consumers. Sender completion remains a lifetime/retirement obligation but leaves the first-MMA dependency chain. Share validation across consumers when its extra local notification costs less than redundant polling.

   This differs from our current withheld row-tail word, which is explicitly published after a full drain, and from the failed generic-load compute gather (kernel ~44→109 µs). It also differs from assuming a TMA's last word implies earlier words arrived. Required proof: sentinel encoding for activation **and SF**, stable data after validation, generic→TMA visibility, and safe generations. Cost to beat: extra L2 reads, certificate traffic and polling pressure. Reject if startup improves but the recovered ~25 µs compute span is lost. [Current sender](python/minisgl/kernel/csrc/deepgemm/deep_gemm/include/deep_gemm/impls/mega_moe_dispatch.cuh:190), [protocol history](CODEX_PROJECT_qwen35_push_comm.md:132).

3. **Prioritize complete first-MMA operand windows, with SF and activation on independent issue paths.**

   Send the SF and activation slices needed by the earliest *set* of compute tiles before unrelated later slices. Give SF and activation separate issuer/group state so SF publication can finish while activation issue continues. Receiver readiness covers a consumable K window, not the entire source row or every pusher. Preserve source-load reuse across destinations.

   Simply moving SF first is already rejected in CuTe's own comments: its extra full wait and CTA barrier held activation behind SF completion. Our serial direct-SF experiment advanced MMA but lengthened compute. The distinguishing change is concurrent issue/completion ownership with no new inter-plane join. Test whether multiple CTA pairs start earlier without starving later K stages; one early tile is insufficient. [CuTe pusher](../cutedsl_megamoe/next/sources/kernel_src/rubin/inference/mega/token_comm_gen_specialized.py:1306), [SF experiments](CODEX_PROJECT_push_dispatch.md:1779).

4. **Treat warp scheduling and memory-queue occupancy as part of the communication protocol.**

   Separate remote issuers, local readiness polling, SF preparation and gather issue so a blocked remote operation does not stop all local progress. Use bounded outstanding windows and rotating readiness probes; preserve warp-uniform control flow. Compare role placements within existing register-allocation groups to determine whether latency-critical roles compete for the same scheduler/issue resources. Hardware warp-to-scheduler placement must be measured, not assumed from source warp IDs.

   CuTe has dedicated/reused token-back roles and adaptive pacing explicitly intended to avoid remote requests blocking local loads/stores. Borrow the concurrency-control principle; its nanosleep constants are not GB200 tuning results. Our 8→16 sender result and divergent-poll regression argue against maximizing issuers blindly. Measure local operand-stage stalls while changing only remote issue pressure; a win must preserve fanout throughput and first-tile readiness. [Pacing](../cutedsl_megamoe/src/token_comm.py:1119), [warp-uniform repair](CODEX_PROJECT_push_dispatch.md:412).

5. **Keep unready work outside the scheduler FIFO.**

   Hold an unready FC2 descriptor outside the work queue and issue a bounded amount of ready FC1 work before probing again. Extend this idea to FC1 dispatch tiles only if metadata storage permits independent tile/expert sealing: source-segment addresses and completion records must make missing contributors explicit. Avoid making a ready expert depend on unrelated dense-prefix/index construction. Preserve identical arithmetic order within each output.

   CuTe's training scheduler implements held-consumer admission and fuses a readiness probe with a producer claim into one cluster transaction. Its Genphase helper publishes sizes before prefixes and sorts experts by load. Those are useful patterns, but load order alone is not readiness order. Our source-ordinal/address-reply experiments already show that removing one remote dependency can leave a later local join exposed. Require bounded deferral and guaranteed producer progress; never let all resident CTAs hold consumers whose producers are unscheduled. [Held consumer](../cutedsl_megamoe/next/sources/kernel_src/rubin/training/mega/bwd_dglu/dglu_mxfp8_fc12_kernel.py:2244), [early sizes](../cutedsl_megamoe/next/sources/kernel_src/rubin/inference/mega/token_comm_gen_specialized.py:1597).

6. **Allocate pipeline capacity by latency domain, and overlap waits only where independent work exists.**

   Explore independent weight and activation/SF stage depths at fixed shared-memory budget. Precompute next-stage coordinates/SF in registers before shared-slot availability; issue activation gather as soon as its own slot and transaction accounting permit. Use next-stage barrier probes while issuing current MMA only if the generated control flow preserves uniform operands and useful instruction overlap.

   CuTe Genphase separates token and weight depths; its split-depth MMA deliberately omits peek-ahead when extra state/control costs outweigh overlap. Our early-gather variants also show that fewer dependencies on paper can produce worse machine scheduling. Preserve the existing working rendezvous unless a replacement establishes the same expected-byte/phase invariant. Start from concurrent startup, which already restores compute, rather than redoing its solved serialization. [Asymmetric stages](../cutedsl_megamoe/next/sources/kernel_src/rubin/inference/mega/block_scaled_swap_ab_fc12_mainloop_gen_specialized.py:94), [peek tradeoff](../cutedsl_megamoe/next/sources/kernel_src/rubin/inference/mega/block_scaled_swap_ab_fc12_mainloop_gen_specialized.py:1420).

7. **Overlap returned-value atomics and metadata loads at their actual dependency boundaries.**

   Issue a necessary slot/work claim before independent initialization, descriptor preparation or other route processing, and consume the returned value only when constructing its dependent address. Prefetch next-tile routing scores/destination addresses before accumulator readiness where their own metadata is already valid. Publish separate input/sizes/index milestones so weight setup need not wait for payload or final layout.

   CuTe explicitly issues the first scheduler claim before cluster initialization waits, and has metadata-prefetch/early-TMEM-release epilogues. FastAFD already gains a small amount from epilogue prefetch; this is residual work, not a new headline idea. A slot-allocation atomic needs its return value and cannot be replaced with a no-return reduction. Early PDL alone previously moved the timing origin and regressed kernel time. Measure from the producer's metadata start and verify useful dependent work actually advances. [Early claim](../cutedsl_megamoe/moe_nvfp4_swapab/kernel_fc12.py:1476), [epilogue lifetime](../cutedsl_megamoe/next/sources/kernel_src/blackwell/inference/mega/block_scaled_swap_ab_fc12_epilogue.py:2173).

8. **Decouple return-store retirement from TMEM release and local reduction admission. Separate follow-on axis.**

   Keep accumulation storage reusable once its required values have been read, retain a bounded output-staging ring, and overlap remote stores with later epilogue work using source-read completion for staging reuse. For Lamport combine, allow another ready vector/token to progress while one contribution is missing, retaining the established top-k summation order. Keep receive-buffer credits/clearing behind actual consumption, outside the next producer's critical path where legal.

   CuTe's staged UBLK epilogue and early accumulator release supply the ownership pattern; its remote reduce-add and reduced-precision wire formats change numerical behavior and are not interchangeable with our fixed combine. Our existing combine already polls multiple top-k slots and overlaps cleanup, so target only remaining vector-level head-of-line waits. Hold combine fixed for items 1–7; do not attribute its redesign to dispatch. [Staged return](../cutedsl_megamoe/next/sources/kernel_src/blackwell/inference/mega/block_scaled_swap_ab_fc12_epilogue.py:2896), [current combine audit](scratch/megamoe/lamport_payload/LATENCY_AUDIT.md:32).

**Execution order if pursued:** 1 and 4 first; 2 as the major protocol branch, then 3 if startup remains dominant. Use 5–7 only against a measured remaining dependency; keep 8 independent. For each comparison retain historical pull and the best concurrent-startup control, fixed EP8/rows32, ordinary kernel and full producer-chain timing, actual first-MMA distribution, and compute span. Sparse sender/receiver markers must distinguish issue, source-read completion, full completion, publication and first use. Cross-GPU timestamps require an aligned time basis; the CuTe P2P benchmark's push clock measures issuer full-wait completion, not receiver arrival. Arbitrary/empty routes and replay remain correctness requirements; reset preparation stays outside kernel timing for both arms.

Review coverage: legacy `src` routing/token-in/token-back/flags; NVFP4 schedulers and epilogues; BF16, Hopper FP8, MXFP8 GLU/BF16/DGLU variants; `next` deterministic/nondeterministic communication, Blackwell/Rubin inference, Rubin Genphase/local routing, training FC12/Wgrad pipelines, scheduler helpers, synchronization, workspace lifetimes, tester knobs and the P2P benchmark. Shared mechanisms are consolidated above. Rubin-specific MMA, cluster dimensions, stage counts and 27-pusher occupancy assumptions are not portable GB200 prescriptions. CuTe source patterns are design evidence, not proof of speedup or a substitute for cross-proxy ordering analysis; in particular, its `.read`-then-flag variants should not be copied as a readiness proof.

**Additional variants and deferred questions retained from the review.** These expand the eight directions above; they are not additional accepted optimizations or authorization to run experiments.

- **Separate source reuse from destination publication queues (1).** Track which committed groups still read each staging slot independently from which destinations can be notified. Peer-specific groups can reduce completion coupling, but `wait_group N` retires an ordered prefix, not an arbitrary chosen group. Truly independent completion may require different issuing threads. A designated completion warp must also own the corresponding issues; handing it another warp's wait is invalid.
- **Warp-per-peer versus warp-per-row assignment (1, 4).** Compare a bounded two-dimensional assignment of rows and destinations while retaining one local load per source row where possible. Several issuers can read shared source staging, but they need an explicit source-lifetime handoff. This differs from the previously losing destination-owned design that repeatedly loaded the same source. Keep CTA count fixed to isolate ownership.
- **Remove only synchronization with a replacement ownership proof (1, 4).** Distinguish the join needed to make shared staging readable, the join needed to reuse it, and the join inherited solely for one publisher's fence. Replace the last kind with local completion records or per-owner publication where justified. Trace the complete downstream chain: earlier experiments removed one join while later joins preserved the same serialization.
- **Exploit existing idle role capacity selectively (4, 7).** Warp7 and a companion CTA's nonissuing MMA role are possible homes for address preparation, readiness probing or SF work. Their register budget and warpgroup allocation rules constrain this; they are not free general-purpose workers. A role permutation should preserve allocation/deallocation progress and avoid adding a new rendezvous to the critical gather path.
- **Batch notifications according to consumption urgency (1, 5).** Publish the first consumable tile immediately; batch noncritical interior notifications only while useful independent work runs; flush tails and phase changes explicitly. CuTe's lane-batched synchronous versus warp-batched asynchronous trackers are distinct mappings. Large batches can withhold the very FC1 completion needed for FC2 progress, so batching must respect the scheduling watermark, not merely reduce atomic count.
- **Validate once, consume many times (2).** Compare distributed validators with a local shared readiness certificate per source-row/K window. This trades duplicated polling across expert consumers against a local publication/acquire and possible certificate contention. Never validate one sentinel word and infer validity of an unordered bulk transfer. Also assess whether keeping validated values for direct staging can avoid a second read without repeating the previous compute-gather regression; this is an alternative branch, not the default TMA-preserving proposal.
- **Schedule by readiness and useful remaining work (3, 5).** Load-descending expert order may improve the tail but delay immediately ready small work. Consider a bounded ready set with load used only among ready candidates. Metadata requires per-source sealing/counts, including zero contributions, before declaring a tile complete. A source-indexed layout can avoid a returned destination address, but arbitrary routing and generation-safe metadata storage remain mandatory.
- **FC1→FC2 readiness by a bounded K window (3, 5, 6).** Starting FC2 before the entire FC1 output tile is ready could overlap phases while preserving each output's K accumulation order. The historical on-demand FC2 scheme already regressed under expert-wave scheduling; revisit only with a changed scheduler or reduced readiness overhead that directly addresses that failure. Do not introduce a full completion round trip for every small chunk.
- **Use peek-ahead selectively (6).** Probe the next operand barrier before current MMA issue, or acquire the next producer slot while current copies progress. More state, predicates and register lifetimes can outweigh the hidden wait, especially with two independent pipelines. Keep compile-time uniform gather operands and ensure a probe does not accidentally become an early blocking wait.
- **Retain cluster sharing where it saves repeated local work (2, 6).** Explore sharing readiness/SF staging or existing TMA multicast within a compute cluster, comparing the handoff cost against duplicated loads. This is GPU-local cluster communication; it does not itself provide cross-GPU payload arrival notification.
- **Remote multicast and hardware-assisted validity: parked research questions (1, 2).** Determine whether the deployed hardware/toolchain offers an appropriate multicast publication or transfer primitive for genuinely common fanout, or a bulk-load validity report that could replace software word scanning. These were not established as usable GB200 solutions in this review. Verify instruction/architecture support, memory mappings, ordering and arbitrary destination masks before making an implementation proposal; neither a cluster mbarrier nor a Rubin-specific primitive is automatically a remote-GPU completion mechanism.
- **Credits and retirement belong on a separate lifetime path (2, 8).** Let ready consumers progress while unrelated senders retire, but retain the required drain before destroying staging or resetting shared state. Grant a future receive generation only after its prior consumers and clearing finish. Double buffering alone is not a reuse proof. A future steady-state credit protocol must include its real overhead in full-chain timing even when the current isolated benchmark keeps preparation outside kernel timing.
- **Progress across combine vectors, not just top-k contributors (8).** The existing poller already batches top-k loads. A missing first vector can still delay a second independent vector owned by the same thread. Explore a small register-resident pending-vector set or separate loader/reducer roles, retaining fixed per-vector top-k accumulation order and checking register pressure. Avoid a rank-wide admission barrier that prevents this local progress.
- **Discriminating measurements before broad tuning.** Separate notification issue→receiver acquire, issuer full-wait duration, all-issuer drain→publication, and receiver-ready→actual MMA. Capture the relevant source and destination rather than interpreting rank0 outgoing markers as incoming causality. Vary outstanding remote work while observing local operand stalls to test queue interference; use matched role assignments to test scheduler contention. A faster standalone push benchmark or an earlier single CTA is insufficient evidence of a faster megakernel.
